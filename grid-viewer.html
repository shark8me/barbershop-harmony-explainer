How to use this for learning chord↔lead association:
  1. Whole tag once, slow. Press ▶ Whole tag at 40–50 bpm. You hear the lead n-gram in context and watch the harmonic journey across columns (fifths) and rows (function).
  2. Loop an n-gram — the default learning move. Pick the top-ranked n-gram for the tag from the dropdown. Looping a 3–5 chord cell makes the lead-note → chord-cell association automatic within a few repetitions.
  3. Step through to interrogate. ‹ › steps one chord; the status line names the roman, its function, and the lead degree singing over it. Click any grid cell to hear that chord alone.
  4. Watch phrase ends. Dashed sequence rows are phrase boundaries; green/amber outlines show whether the phrase resolved or stalled in tension — the ear learns “tension wants to move, resolution can rest”.
  5. Then transpose. The grid collapses to three columns — [IV ⟨tr⟩ | tonic | V ⟨tr⟩]. The side columns are the tag's own sequence transposed up a 4th/5th (dimmed, italic): the same n-gram pattern appears shifted, so a V7→I cadence reads as a two-step walk across columns. Click a side cell to audition the transposed chord. Switch to the full 11-tonic view to compare tags in other keys.
